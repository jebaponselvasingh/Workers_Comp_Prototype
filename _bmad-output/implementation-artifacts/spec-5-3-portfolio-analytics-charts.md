## Intent

**Problem:** The dashboard ends after ten KPI cards and a handler table. A supervisor can read that 32 claims are high risk but not *where* the risk, the cost and the delay concentrate — no distribution of stage, severity band, recovery status, injury type, employer spend or state exists as a server aggregate, the four SLA figures exist only in the top bar, and the SPA has no charting library at all.

**Approach:** One new scoped aggregate, `services/worklist/charts.py`, takes a single widened read over the caller's claims — projected as `[*sla.SAMPLE_COLUMNS, …]` so the SLA columns stay inside their one allowlisted module — and folds it into six finished distribution series plus the SLA strip, computed by the *same* `sla.strip_of` the top bar calls. `GET /dashboard/charts` serves them. In `web/`, Recharts 3 arrives with its first consumer and renders geometry only: every count, every ordering, every truncation and every threshold on screen came off the wire.

## Boundaries & Constraints

**Always:**
- The severity donut counts bands with `derivations.risk.for_thresholds(thresholds)` — the same registered derivation and the same document Story 5.1's High Risk card used. Its High slice must equal that card's `high_risk` on the same scope, asserted as an equality between the two aggregates, not restated as a number.
- The settlement-status donut keys on **`Claim.stage`**, not `Claim.status`. Story 5.1 ruled on exactly this (`summary.py` L151-157): `stage = settled` is 62 of the seeded 100 where `status = settled_closed` is 54, and `stage` is what every other surface groups by. A donut on `status` beside a card on `stage` would contradict itself on one screen. Its `settled` slice must equal `settled_closed`, its `treatment` slice `under_treatment`, and its total `total_claims`.
- SLA tiles come from `sla.strip_of` over `sla.sample_of(row)` for the same scoped rows — one fold, four `SlaMetric`s, published with the value/target/direction/decimals/status the top bar already publishes. The dashboard tiles and `GET /stats/sla` are two renderings of one server value and must be asserted equal field-for-field.
- Total paid per employer sums `derivations.total_paid.for_thresholds(thresholds).of(claim)` — the same derivation as Story 5.1's Total Paid card, whose known exclusion of `status = paid` bills is a recorded, owned deferral. The employer series' paid total across all rows must equal `portfolio_summary`'s `total_paid_cents` on the same scope.
- Every read is one `claims` repository call carrying `ctx`; the scope predicate is unconditional; no role branch anywhere in the service path (AD-7). Handlers, employers and states are derived from in-scope claims, never from a roster — an employer with no claim in the caller's book does not appear.
- Rule parameters are loaded in the router and injected into the aggregate as arguments, never fetched inside it (`summary.py` / `benchmarks.py` convention).
- `services/worklist/charts.py` and `api/routers/dashboard.py` may not contain a bare `65` or `35` anywhere, comments and docstrings included (`test_no_module_outside_the_registry_hardcodes_the_band`), nor the strings `sla_pick_days` / `sla_approve_days` / `settlement_days` (`test_nothing_outside_the_worklist_aggregation_reads_the_sla_source_columns`).
- The browser computes no count, no percentage, no ordering, no truncation and no "other" bucket. Recharts is permitted to compute pixel geometry from finished values — that is what a chart library is — but no percentage, ratio or label may reach the screen that a service did not produce.
- Every chart surface renders a fixed-height container in all four states (loading, data, empty, error), so layout never collapses (NFR-3). Errors replace the surface's content with `role="alert"`; they never render stale data beside an alert and never open a dialog.

**Block If:**
- The seeded `injury_type` or `state` values turn out to need normalization (trimming, case-folding, merging near-duplicates) to produce a sensible ranking. Grouping identity is a data-quality decision, not a dev-time correction — HALT rather than inventing a canonicalization.
- Recharts 3.x cannot be installed against React 19 / Vite 8 without a peer-dependency override. Adding an override to force a library in is a shared-plumbing decision — HALT with the resolution output.

**Never:**
- No second SLA aggregation, no second `total_paid`, no second severity band, no re-averaging of anything `strip_of` already returned.
- No new table, no new migration, no new rule document, no write path, no audit event. Reads only.
- No segment-click handlers, no drill-through, no URL state — that is Story 5.5. Components take finished series as props so 5.5 can attach handlers without a rewrite, and nothing more.
- No top-30 worklist (5.4), no analyst-only dimension, no export, no cohorting (Epic 7).
- No palette invention: series colours are the Story 1.1 tokens plus the prototype's five extension hues, and nothing else.
- No `label`/`percent` render prop on a Recharts `Pie`, no `.sort(`, `.reduce(`, `.filter(` over series in `features/`, no numeric-literal comparison in a chart component.

## Design Notes

**1. The donut titled "Settlement status" keys on `stage`, and that is Story 5.1's ruling, not a new one.** `summary.py` L151-157 already decided this for the cards: `stage = settled` counts 62 of the seeded 100 where `status = settled_closed` counts 54, and `stage` is what the queue's four groups, the SLA aggregation and the KPI cards all group by. The prototype's `renderSV` is internally inconsistent here — it computes `settled` from `status` and `active` from `stage` in the same expression — and porting that inconsistency would put a 54 in a donut sitting inches from a card reading 62. The donut keeps the prototype's heading and legend wording; only its source column is corrected, and the two cross-surface equality tests are what stop it drifting back.

**2. Absent categories are omitted, not zeroed, and that is a contract the UI must be built against.** Jennifer Park's 27 claims contain no `intake` stage and no `initial` or `denied` status. Emitting `{key: "intake", count: 0}` would put an invisible slice in a donut and a zero-length bar in a chart; omitting it means the series lists exactly what the scope contains. The cost is that the UI cannot assume a fixed row count, which is why the label maps are `Record<Enum, string>` lookups over whatever arrived rather than a fixed `TILES`-style array. The empty-scope case is the same rule taken to its limit: `items: []`, not seven zero rows.

**3. The two free-text dimensions need a tie-break written down, because the seed splits ties at both boundaries.** `injury_type` and `state` are `Text` columns with no enum and no reference table. On the full portfolio, injury ranks 7-11 are all count 5 and state ranks 9-11 are all count 5 — so both the top-8 and the top-10 cut land inside a tie, and any implementation that leaves ordering to the dict or the database will produce a different chart on a different day. The rule is count descending, then label ascending, applied in the pure fold and property-tested. Grouping is on the stored value with no normalization: trimming or case-folding would be a data-quality decision, and the Block-If says HALT rather than invent one.

**4. Recharts computes geometry; it does not compute figures.** AD-1 forbids the SPA aggregating, truncating, ranking or percentaging raw claims — it does not forbid a chart library turning a finished value into an arc. The line this story draws: values, ordering, truncation, totals and thresholds arrive finished; Recharts maps them to pixels; anything that would print a number the server did not send is banned by name (`Pie`'s `label`/`percent` props, `LabelList` with a formatter that does arithmetic, any `.sort`/`.reduce`/`.filter` in `features/`). The prototype's donut legend shows counts and its centre shows the scope total — no percentage appears anywhere in the design contract, which is what makes this line easy to hold.

**5. The visible chart is `aria-hidden` and every surface carries an `sr-only` list of its label/value pairs.** This is `RiskGauge`'s pattern (`role="img"` + `aria-label`, inner `<text aria-hidden>`) extended from one value to a series, and it does three jobs at once: it is the accessible rendering of a chart, it is what Playwright and Vitest assert against, and it means the tests do not depend on Recharts' internal SVG structure or on jsdom measuring anything. The jsdom size seam in `setup.ts` is still needed — a chart that renders nothing in every test would let a broken `ResponsiveContainer` ship — but it is a backstop assertion (`an <svg> exists`) rather than the substance of the tests.

**6. Ungated, and the discriminator is whether a named person's performance is on the wire.** The two dashboard endpoints now disagree: `/dashboard/summary` serves any authenticated caller (tested, and recorded as a deferral that Story 5.2 was told to decide on its own terms), while `/dashboard/handler-benchmarks` refuses everyone outside a supervisor/analyst allowlist. Neither precedent binds by seniority; the principled split is that benchmarks rank *colleagues by name and speed*, which is a capability a handler does not have, whereas these seven series describe the caller's own book by stage, severity, injury type, employer and state and name nobody. Scope already guarantees a handler sees only her own claims. So charts follow `/summary`, and the decision is a route docstring and a test (`test_a_handler_may_read_her_own_books_charts`), not an accident.

**7. Top-8 and top-10 are module constants, not a rule document, and they travel on the wire.** AD-8 puts "weights, thresholds, bands, caps" in JDM, and the epic context names which of Epic 5's numbers are rules-tier: the risk and fraud thresholds, the cycle-time bands, the worklist cap, the complexity weights. A chart's category count is none of those — it is UX-DR7's shape ("top 8", "top 10"), and moving it would change what the chart *is*, not how the business behaves. A new document plus a migration plus a loader plus validation for two integers the design contract fixes would be ceremony, so they are `Final` constants with the reasoning in a comment. What they must not be is invisible: `limit`, `totalCategories` and `truncated` ship on every ranked series so the caption can say "8 of 20" and no client has to restate the number.

**8. Total Paid inherits a known, owned gap — deliberately.** `TotalPaidDerivation` sums the three static `paid_*` columns and excludes $335,985 of `status = paid` bills and expenses that the Bills tab shows; 38 of 100 seeded claims carry paid bills with zero paid columns. That is recorded from Story 5.1 as wanting a product owner, not a dev-time correction. The employer bars use the same derivation anyway, because the alternative is a dashboard showing two different "total paid" figures on one screen. The equality test against `total_paid_cents` is what makes the choice load-bearing instead of incidental: if someone later fixes the derivation, both surfaces move together or the test fails.
